I. OUR CONVICTION

BEYOND THE TOOLKIT

Business is a human discipline
before it is a technical one.

Organisations are shaped by judgement, institutions, incentives, culture and the choices people make together. Serious management education must examine all of them.

How we learn ↗

02 / JUDGEMENT

Act with purpose.

Translate sound analysis into responsible decisions, clear communication and considered action.

PROGRAMME 01 · STRATEGY

Strategic
Management.

Understand how organisations analyse their environments, choose a direction and build the capabilities required to sustain performance.

FIELDStrategy & organisationLEVELAdvanced introductory

Learning outline

  1. Strategy as a field of inquiryPurpose, scope, levels of strategy and the limits of strategic planning.
  2. External analysisIndustry structure, competitive forces, macro-environment and uncertainty.
  3. Resources and capabilitiesResource-based perspectives, dynamic capabilities and organisational fit.
  4. Corporate and business strategyScope, diversification, positioning, business models and strategic choice.
  5. Implementation and renewalOrganisation design, strategic change, execution and evaluation.

Questions to consider

  • What makes an advantage sustainable rather than temporary?
  • How should leaders respond when evidence is incomplete?
  • When can a successful strategy become a source of organisational inertia?

Suggested academic starting points

Use university library access to consult peer-reviewed research and recognised textbooks. Begin with the strategy and management research collections at Oxford Saïd, LSE and Rotterdam School of Management. Check publication details and editions before citing a work.

This is an independent learning resource, not an accredited qualification. A complete lesson sequence, assessed activities and verified bibliography are not yet published.

PROGRAMME 02 · ORGANISATIONS

Organisational
Behaviour.

Explore how individuals and groups behave at work, how organisations create culture, and how leadership shapes collective action.

FIELDPeople & organisationsLEVELAdvanced introductory

Learning outline

  1. Foundations of organisational behaviourLevels of analysis, evidence, context and competing perspectives.
  2. Individual differences and motivationPerception, personality, motivation and work design.
  3. Groups, teams and powerGroup dynamics, conflict, influence, negotiation and decision-making.
  4. Leadership and organisational cultureLeadership approaches, values, norms and culture in practice.
  5. Change, wellbeing and responsibilityOrganisational change, employee experience, inclusion and ethical practice.

Questions to consider

  • How do formal structures and informal norms interact?
  • Why can the same incentive motivate one person and discourage another?
  • What responsibilities do organisations have for employee wellbeing?

Suggested academic starting points

Explore management and organisational research from LSE, Oxford Saïd and Rotterdam School of Management. Prioritise peer-reviewed articles and check the original publication details.

PROGRAMME 03 · FINANCE

Corporate
Finance.

Examine how firms make investment and financing decisions, assess value and balance financial return with risk.

FIELDFinance & investmentLEVELAdvanced introductory

Learning outline

  1. Financial objectives and governanceFirm objectives, agency questions, financial statements and the role of finance.
  2. Time value of money and valuationDiscounting, present value, cash flows and valuation principles.
  3. Investment appraisalNet present value, internal rate of return, capital budgeting and sensitivity.
  4. Risk, return and cost of capitalRisk measurement, diversification, required return and financing costs.
  5. Capital structure and payoutDebt and equity, financing choices, dividends and financial resilience.

Questions to consider

  • Why is cash flow central to investment appraisal?
  • How do assumptions affect a valuation?
  • When might a financially attractive project still be strategically unwise?

Suggested academic starting points

Consult finance research and teaching resources from LSE, Oxford Saïd and HEC Paris. Use current editions of established corporate finance texts and verify all data used in exercises.

Marketing
Strategy.

Study how organisations understand markets, create customer value and build meaningful, defensible positions.

FIELDMarkets & customersLEVELAdvanced introductory

Learning outline

  2. Market and customer analysisResearch design, segmentation, customer insight and decision journeys.

PROGRAMME 05 · ECONOMICS

Managerial
Economics.

Apply economic reasoning to business choices involving incentives, prices, competition, resources and uncertainty.

FIELDEconomics & decisionsLEVELAdvanced introductory

Learning outline

  1. Economic reasoning for managersScarcity, opportunity cost, marginal analysis and models.
  2. Demand, supply and elasticityMarket equilibrium, responsiveness, shifts and practical interpretation.
  3. Production and costsProduction functions, cost structures, scale and productivity.
  4. Market structure and competitionPerfect competition, monopoly, oligopoly and strategic interaction.
  5. Pricing and decisions under uncertaintyPricing logic, information, risk and behavioural limits.

Questions to consider

  • What does opportunity cost reveal that accounting cost may hide?
  • How does market power affect pricing and consumer welfare?
  • When are simplified economic models useful, and when are they dangerous?

Suggested academic starting points

Use university economics teaching and research from LSE, University of Oxford and HEC Paris. Check model assumptions and cite the original academic material used.

Governance.

Learning outline

  1. Purpose and governance systemsOwnership, control, accountability and institutional context.
